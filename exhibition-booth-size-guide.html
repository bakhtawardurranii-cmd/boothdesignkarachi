Exhibition booth size guide: how big should your stall be?

Booking space before you know what fits is the costly mistake. This guide shows the common booth sizes, the size used at a recent Expo Centre Karachi show, and how to pick yours from what your stall must do.

Start a project Common questions

Quick answer

There is no single standard exhibition booth size. Common modules are 9 sqm (3×3 m), 12 sqm (4×3 m) and 18 sqm (6×3 m). At TEXPO-24 at Expo Centre Karachi the standard booth was 12 sqm with a 3 m height, so check your organiser's layout plan before you design. Choose by what must happen inside: one focused activity fits 9 sqm, and more than one at a time calls for a larger plot.

What are the standard exhibition booth sizes?

Booth sizes are set by each organiser, so these are common starting points, not rules.

Choosing

What size exhibition stall do you actually need?

Start from the work the stall must do, not from the price of the space. Count the activities that must happen at the same time, such as a product demo, a meeting, a waiting visitor and someone refilling brochures.

  • One activity (show a sample, take an enquiry): a 3×3 m stall is a workable start.
  • Two or more at once (demo plus meeting): test a larger plot such as 6×3 m, or simplify the brief.
  • Storage and staff: put storage access and staff positions on the plan from the start.
  • Always draw it to scale with your product, counter and visitor space before you book.

Position

Corner, inline and island booths: how position changes the size you need

Two booths with the same area can behave very differently. At TEXPO-24, a corner booth had two sides visible and an inline (liner) booth had one (exhibitor manual). An island is open on four sides and is not a fixed size.

  • Inline: one open face, so put the strongest message and entrance there.
  • Corner: two open faces, so brand both and avoid blocking either with storage.
  • Island: design for every approach; there is no single back wall.

Usable space

How much of your booth can you actually use?

Your footprint is not all usable. Frame members and fascia can reduce internal space, so check the usable dimensions of any panel system (sizing guide). At TEXPO-24 the shell walls were 1 m wide panels, 3 m high, with 2.75 m visible (exhibitor manual).

Also ask what the package already includes. One TDAP shell scheme listed back and side walls, a name fascia, a power point, spotlights and basic furniture (TDAP shell scheme details). Shell contents vary by show, so confirm yours before adding furniture of your own.

Rules

Shell scheme or raw space: how size rules differ

In raw space, the exhibitor is primarily responsible for building the stall. In a shell scheme, the organiser supplies the walls and you design the display inside them. The TEXPO-24 manual also set rules that affect your design (read the manual):

  • No glue, nails or staples on shell walls. Wooden frames carrying flex or vinyl were allowed.
  • Only minor changes to a shell stand were possible on setup day.
  • Switching between raw space and shell scheme after the deadline cost an extra Rs. 10,000.
  • Set sizes may vary in a few cases, so check the show's layout plan.

These rules belong to that show and year. Your organiser's current manual is the one that counts. See raw space vs shell scheme at Expo Centre Karachi and the shows coming up at the venue.

Budget

Does booth size change the price?

Yes. Size is one of the main drivers, together with space type, materials, branding and timeline. A larger stall also usually needs more build and setup time.
