Decision factors

Shell scheme design vs. raw space design

Both are exhibition stall design — the difference is what you're starting from.

Shell Scheme

Working within the organizer's frame

Most exhibition organizers provide a basic shell — walls, fascia and sometimes lighting. Design work covers what goes inside and on top of it: counters, graphics, furniture layout and branding, so a standard booth still reads as yours.

Raw Space

Designing from an empty floor

No shell provided — just a marked floor area. The design covers structure, height, walkways and sightlines from scratch. This is where a stand can look genuinely different from the row next to it.

Stall or booth?

Stall design and booth design: what changes with the position you're given

Karachi organisers use both words, often interchangeably. The space you are allocated matters more than the label.

Stall design

Compact, row-based, inside the organiser's shell

In Karachi, "stall" usually means a fixed row position at a trade fair: a basic organiser-provided shell with walls and fascia, from a single unit up to a few metres wide. Design works inside that frame — counters, product displays, wall graphics, lighting and signage — arranged so visitors walking the row actually stop. It is typically faster and more budget-controlled than a full booth build.

Booth design

Larger, more open, often seen from several sides

"Booth" usually implies more space and more freedom: an island or corner position, or a larger raw-space allocation. There is no fixed size cutoff — position and openness matter more than square footage. The design has to work without a single "front", and often fits meeting areas, storage and separate product zones into one layout.

Stall priorities

Visitor flow in a narrow footprint

Counter placement, product display height, fascia branding and lighting decide whether someone walking the aisle stops. Because the shell is fixed, distinctiveness comes from graphics, lighting, counter design and product placement rather than structure.

Booth priorities

Layout, sightlines and approvals

Open sides need branding and sightlines on every face. Larger or structurally complex booths — especially raw-space builds — often need structural sign-off from the organiser or venue; standard shell-scheme stalls usually don't.

If a booth needs a double-storey structure, an irregular footprint or a fully bespoke build, it becomes a custom exhibition stand. For what drives the price at either scale, see exhibition stall cost in Karachi.
